§About this publication

This document publishes the complete technical characteristics of weakmail, so that anyone can build a transmitter, receiver or monitor for it from this text alone. It defines, normatively, the modulation (tones, symbol timing, modulation index, preambles), the frame format and every encoding used in it, the error-correcting code including the construction of its parity-check matrices and interleavers, the authentication tags, the mailbox protocol and the downlink waveforms, and it gives test vectors that pin every one of them to exact bits.

It is published before any on-air use, as the public documentation of the technique that 47 CFR §97.309(a)(4) refers to and that §97.309(b) and §97.113(a)(4) presuppose: a code anyone can decode does not obscure the meaning of a message. Nothing in weakmail is encrypted. Every header, every message body and every protocol field is sent in the clear in an encoding defined here; cryptography is used only to authenticate (Ed25519 signatures and HMAC tags appended to plain text) and to gate which station may ask a mailbox to retransmit a stored message. Anyone who receives a weakmail transmission can read it.

The sections numbered 0 to 7 and Appendix A are the specification itself, reproduced from the reference implementation's docs/SPEC.md with no change to the text. Where they name a file (weakmail/params.py, tests/...) it is a file of that reference implementation. Sections marked informative describe the reference receiver and its measured performance; a conforming receiver may work differently. The summary below and appendices E, R and V were added for this publication; their numbers are computed from the reference implementation's constants by tools/build_spec.py.

Status. Draft v1. The format is complete and implemented, and has been tested against simulated signals only; it is not yet in use on the air. Any change to what is transmitted will be published here, with a new version number, before it is used.
Open items in this draft, to be settled before the affected parts are used on the air:
  • Key-ups longer than 10 minutes. A single frame can take longer than the 10-minute identification interval of §97.119(a): WEAK n ≥ 4302 (12.0 min and more), WEAK-X n ≥ 2394 (13.4 min and more). The callsign in such a frame's header cannot be decoded until the frame ends, and v1 defines no identification inside a burst. Until it does, a station should not send frames whose key-up exceeds 10 minutes.
  • Identification of sending stations. Every frame carries the sender's callsign (2.3), and mailboxes also identify in CW (4.12). Whether the header alone satisfies §97.119(b) for a handheld is discussed in Appendix R; a sending station can close each transmission with a CW or voice identification.
  • BEACON waveform (1.4). The on-off-keyed carrier is defined as a waveform only: its bit content, framing and identification are not specified. It is used for hardware tests and must not be used on the air as a weakmail transmission until specified. (The BEACON frame type of 4.13 is a normal frame and is fully specified.)

Anyone may implement this specification. The reference implementation (Python) and these pages (JavaScript, including a port of the encoder and receiver checked bit for bit against the reference) are free software under the GNU General Public License, version 3 or later.

§0. No encryption

Nothing in weakmail is encrypted, and no part of it may ever be. FCC Part 97 §97.113(a)(4) prohibits "messages encoded for the purpose of obscuring their meaning". weakmail therefore:

  • sends every header and body in the clear, using encodings published here (FT8 callsign packing, a fixed 6-bit alphabet, UTF-8, or raw bytes);
  • uses cryptography only for authentication: an Ed25519 signature or an HMAC tag is appended to plain-text content that anyone can decode and read without any key;
  • gates retrieval with a signed nonce (access control), not by hiding content — anyone listening can read a delivered message;
  • carries no hash-only commitments of content (a hash in place of the text would obscure meaning).

The same position holds under ITU RR 25.2A (authentication yes, confidentiality no) and Canadian RBR-4 (published non-secret codes). See the design document, "Regulatory", for the full analysis and the open-risk entry on §97.113(a)(4).

§1. Physical layer

This section defines what a weakmail transmitter puts on the air. The reference implementation is weakmail/params.py (constants), weakmail/mfsk.py (synthesis) and weakmail/modem.py (frame → waveform). Section 1.9 describes a reference receiver, which is informative only.

§1.1 Signal model

The sender plays an audio tone sequence into an ordinary FM handheld's microphone input. The HT applies 6 dB/octave pre-emphasis, so its deviation is proportional to tone frequency, and pre-emphasised FM with a constant modulation index is phase modulation. The RF signal at complex baseband (carrier at 0 Hz, unit power) is

s(t) = exp( j · β · sin ψ(t) ),     dψ/dt = 2π · f(t)

where f(t) is the tone frequency of the current symbol and ψ is continuous across symbol boundaries. A symbol on tone k produces RF lines at 0, ±f_k and ±2f_k. Their power fractions are J₀(β)², J₁(β)² (each side) and J₂(β)² (each side), plus negligible higher orders.

§1.2 Tone plan and modulation index

item value
tones 8, f_k = 1300 + 160·k Hz, k = 0…7 (1300, 1460, …, 2420 Hz)
bits per symbol 3
modulation index β 1.5 for every tone (2.0–3.6 kHz peak deviation over the tone set)
line powers at β = 1.5 J₀² = 0.262 (carrier/pilot), J₁² = 0.311 each at ±f_k, J₂² = 0.054 each at ±2f_k

Design rules behind these numbers (design doc, "Physical layer"):

  • No collisions. 2 · 1300 > 2420, so no second-order line ±2f_j ever falls on a first-order line ±f_k.
  • Within the HT passband. All tones avoid the HT's dead band below 300 Hz and its roll-off above 3 kHz.
  • Pilot carrier. β must stay well below 2.405, where J₀ = 0 and the carrier the receiver tracks disappears. β = 1.0 costs about 3 dB and β = 0.5 about 8 dB.
  • Constant β needs calibration. The sender drives the microphone at 30–50 % of full deviation (reference amplitude 0.4 of full scale), so the limiter never clips. Each radio needs its own drive-level calibration (test E2), optionally as an 8-entry per-tone gain table. Radios or data ports without pre-emphasis scale tone amplitude ∝ f_k (preemph_comp) to keep β constant.

§1.3 Gray mapping

Coded bits are taken 3 at a time, MSB first, as a label v = 4·b₀ + 2·b₁ + b₂. Label v is sent on the tone k whose Gray code is v:

GRAY[k]   = k XOR (k >> 1)     tone k carries label GRAY[k]
k         : 0 1 2 3 4 5 6 7
GRAY[k]   : 0 1 3 2 6 7 5 4
tone of v : v=0→0, 1→1, 2→3, 3→2, 4→7, 5→6, 6→4, 7→5

Adjacent tones therefore differ in exactly one bit. The coded bit stream (section 3, after interleaving) is always a multiple of 3 bits long.

  • BEACON: PTT on-off keying of a bare carrier, 1-s bits (bit 1 = carrier on), with raised-cosine edges of 50 ms (weakmail/ook.py).

§1.5 Preambles

Every MFSK burst starts with 8 preamble symbols. The pattern identifies the class and gives frame sync; there are no UTC time slots.

class preamble tone indices (first symbol first)
WEAK (0) 0 5 2 7 4 1 6 3
WEAK-X (1) 3 6 1 4 7 2 5 0
MOBILE (2) 7 2 4 1 3 6 0 5

Each pattern uses every tone exactly once, which lets a receiver estimate the per-line J-ratios (β) from the preamble. The WEAK-X pattern is the WEAK pattern reversed. The two are still well separated, because they also differ in symbol length: in the reference receiver the right class scores 2.5–6× higher than the wrong one.

§1.6 Burst structure and keying

PTT key-up
│← TX delay →│← 8 preamble symbols →│← n/3 data symbols →│ PTT release
   silence       class pattern         Gray-mapped coded bits (section 3)
   (bare carrier on air)
  • TX delay: 0.4 s of silence between PTT key-up and the first preamble symbol. The allowed range is 0.3–0.5 s, and the HT radiates an unmodulated carrier during it. The squelch tail and VOX hang come after the frame and are not part of it.
  • Phase and ramps: the audio is continuous-phase from the first preamble symbol to the last data symbol, with no gaps between symbols. ψ starts at 0. A 10 ms raised-cosine ramp at the start and end of the tone burst prevents key clicks. Discretely, for a tone burst of n samples at rate fs, with N = min(round(0.010 · fs), ⌊n/2⌋): env[k] = 0.5 · (1 − cos(π (k + 0.5) / N)) for k = 0 … N−1, env[n−1−k] = env[k] (mirrored over the last N samples) and 1 elsewhere. It multiplies the tone burst only, never the TX-delay silence before it (normative for the audio test vectors, 1.8).
  • Data: the data symbols are the n coded bits of one frame, n/3 symbols, where n is the frame's size class (section 3.2). The burst carries no explicit length field. The receiver infers n from the burst duration and from the code itself (section 3.4).
  • Airtime (section 3.5) = TX delay + 8·T + (n/3)·T. For example, the signed 32-character SUBMIT is 0.4 + 4 + 271 = 275.4 s in WEAK, and the unsigned one is 0.4 + 1 + 25 = 26.4 s in MOBILE.
  • One frame per key-up is the reference behaviour. A receiver must cope with several bursts in one recording: short bursts in a long recording, bursts at different carrier offsets, back-to-back bursts and bursts overlapping in time at different offsets.
  • Sender clock. The sound card's sample clock sets both the symbol length and the tone frequencies (a clock eps fast plays symbols of T/(1+eps) at f_k(1+eps)). Senders should stay within ±1000 ppm; the reference receiver estimates eps up to ±1000 ppm when a frame fails to decode with nominal timing (1.9). Uncorrected, 300 ppm slips 16 % of a WEAK symbol over a 275-s frame and costs about 1 dB near threshold.

§1.7 Frequency agility

Transmitters need no frequency correction. The receiver must find the carrier anywhere within ±2.5 kHz of the channel centre (Baofeng ±2.5 ppm at 440 MHz) and must tolerate drift of at least 10 Hz/s. The design assumption is a smooth quadratic f(t) over a burst (hardware test E3); the reference receiver also follows smooth non-quadratic drift such as an exponential HT warm-up (1.9). Stay on standard 12.5 or 25 kHz channel centres.

§1.8 Reference synthesis (normative for test vectors)

  • Audio: mfsk.audio(tones, wc, fs=48000, amplitude=0.4) is float32: 0.4 s of zeros, then amplitude · env[k] · sin ψ(t), with the discrete ramp env of section 1.6 (N = 480 at 48 kS/s), written as a 16-bit mono WAV by weakmail tx. The audio vectors' head window covers the ramp, so reproducing its hash needs exactly this env.
  • IQ reference: mfsk.iq(tones, wc, β, fs=24000, tx_delay_s=0.4) is exp(jβ sin ψ) preceded by 0.4 s of unit carrier. This is what an ideal HT radiates and what the simulations use.

§1.9 Reference receiver (informative)

The mailbox decodes recorded IQ in two passes and never FM-demodulates (weakmail.modem.receive):

  1. Track the carrier. On the 24 kS/s channel, the carrier (pilot) line is tracked with 2-s Hann FFTs stepped by one symbol and a Viterbi ridge. The ridge is first gated to its strongest contiguous stretch (a short burst in a long record must not be out-voted by noise windows), then fitted with a robust quadratic and refined by de-chirp-and-re-track; clearly non-quadratic drift adds a cubic smoothing spline of the residual (30-s knots). A symmetric-pair score and a whole-burst centre-of-symmetry check over the carrier, the first-order and the second-order line pairs keep it off the sidebands up to β = 2 with a repeated tone. A Doppler fold (centre of symmetry of the burst-integrated comb, ±30 Hz) moves the curve from one Jakes horn to the line centre under fading. The tracker's lock statistic rejects noise-only records. A locked carrier with no preamble (a spur, birdie, kerchunk or FM voice) is masked and the record re-tracked (up to 3 carriers), and a MOBILE preamble that does not decode is retried with the 0.5-s MOBILE window.
  2. De-chirp the record with the fitted carrier phase.
  3. Find the preamble. Search for each class's preamble within ±4 s of the tracked burst start (1/8-symbol grid refined to 1/64 symbol). The score is a noise-calibrated z-statistic, and a burst needs z ≥ 6 and at least 4 of its 8 symbols with the pattern tone strongest (a scale-free check: z grows with SNR for any comb-like content, so a strong burst of one class can out-score with another class's pattern). Classes passing both are ranked by the mean share of each preamble symbol's comb energy on its pattern tone and decoded in that order until one passes.
  4. Choose length candidates. The tracker's burst end gives the expected number of data symbols. Size classes within ±3 s of airtime of it are tried first (nearest first), then all others that fit.
  5. Compute tone metrics. For each symbol and each tone, J₁²·(P(+f_k) + P(−f_k)) + J₂²·(P(+2f_k) + P(−2f_k)), where each P is the maximum power over ±1 bin of a rectangular one-symbol FFT. MOBILE's FFT is zero-padded 2× so its lines fall on bins.
  6. Decode. Soft bits: a steady per-tone background (median over symbols above the global median) is removed, max-log Gray LLRs are clipped to 5× their median magnitude, and symbols whose second-strongest tone is far outside its distribution (an impulse or a carrier on another tone) are erased. They feed the LDPC decoder (60 iterations, normalised min-sum, α = 0.8, input LLRs saturated as in 3.4), then CRC-16 and the frame checks of section 3.4. If no length candidate decodes, the sender clock error is estimated (±1000 ppm, 1.6) and the data symbols are re-demodulated at the corrected timing and tone frequencies. If that fails too, other detectors are tried on the same burst (modem.DETECTOR_HYPOTHESES; a frame the square-law metric decodes is never affected): the four lines of each tone combined coherently (maximised over the unknown audio phase), keeping the ±1-bin maximum, on the same preamble; then, unless the carrier is a narrow line, a Doppler pass for a moving sender (fD = 10 and 25 Hz): the record is re-centred block by block on the carrier's Doppler-spread spectrum, each line's power is a Jakes-weighted sum over ±(fD·T + a few) bins with the lines combined coherently, and the preamble is searched again with that metric. The decoder metadata names the detector that decoded the frame.
  7. Verify the tag (section 5), then hand the frame and decoder metadata (class, C/N0, offset, drift) to the mailbox (section 4.2).
  8. Continue. The segment is split at the decoded frame's exact extent (TX delay and 2 symbols before the preamble, 2 symbols after the last data symbol), and the frame's own span is searched once more with its carrier and comb masked, for a burst overlapping it in time at another offset. Results are de-duplicated by msg_id. There is no frame cap; the work is bounded by a search budget proportional to the record length (4 × the number of shortest frames that fit, at least 16 attempts), and the receiver warns if the budget runs out with segments left unsearched.

§2. Frame information bits

A frame's information bits (the input to the FEC of section 3) are:

header (92) ‖ body (6·N or 8·N) ‖ CRC-16 (16) ‖ tag (0 / 64 / 384 / 512)

followed by zero padding up to the code's information length k (section 3). A receiver MUST reject a frame whose padding bits are not all zero.

§2.1 Header (92 bits)

body_kind: 0 = 6-bit text (section 2.4), 1 = UTF-8 text, 2 = binary bytes, 3 = reserved (reject).

Field bit offsets within the header: version 0–1, type 2–5, tag kind 6–7, source 8–35, destination 36–63, timestamp 64–79, flags 80–83, body length 84–91.

The tag length is implied by the tag-kind field: 0, 8, 48 or 64 bytes.

Timestamp unwrapping. A receiver reconstructs the full minute count as the value congruent to the 16-bit field (mod 65536) nearest its own clock: the unique value within [−32768, +32767] minutes of the receiver clock (so a field exactly 32768 minutes away resolves to the earlier value). Mailboxes reject frames outside ±2 days (2880 min) of their clock, well inside that unambiguous range of about ±32768 minutes (~22.7 days).

§2.3 Callsign packing (c28)

Callsigns use the 28-bit "c28" packing of FT8/FT4 (WSJT-X packjt77.f90, pack28 / unpack28), restricted to tokens and standard callsigns:

c28 value meaning
0 DE
1 QRZ
2 CQ — in weakmail written ANY: the "any mailbox" destination wildcard
3 … 2 063 591 FT8 CQ nnn / CQ ABCD tokens — not used, reject
2 063 592 … 6 257 895 FT8 22-bit hashed callsigns — not used, reject
6 257 896 … 2²⁸−1 standard callsigns

Constants: NTOKENS = 2063592, MAX22 = 4194304.

A standard callsign consists of A–Z and 0–9 only and satisfies, with the call-area digit defined as the last digit in the call:

  1. the call-area digit is at position 2 or 3 (1-based);
  2. the characters before it contain at least one letter (so are not all digits);
  3. at most 3 characters follow it (all letters, by rule 1's definition).

Examples: K1ABC, W9XYZ, VE3ABC, G4ABC, 2E0ABC, KH6XX, JA1XYZ, 9A1A, W1A. Not standard (rejected): K1ABC/P, K1ABCD, AB12CD, 11ABC.

Two FT8 prefix work-arounds are applied before packing and undone after unpacking:

  • 3DA0… (Eswatini) is packed as 3D0…;
  • 3X followed by a letter (Guinea) is packed as Q + the rest (3XY1AB → QY1AB).

Packing: form a 6-character string with the call-area digit at position 3 — prepend one space if the digit is at position 2 — and pad on the right with spaces. With

A1 = " 0123456789ABCDEFGHIJKLMNOPQRSTUVWXYZ"   (37)  position 1
A2 = "0123456789ABCDEFGHIJKLMNOPQRSTUVWXYZ"    (36)  position 2
A3 = "0123456789"                              (10)  position 3
A4 = " ABCDEFGHIJKLMNOPQRSTUVWXYZ"             (27)  positions 4, 5, 6

and iN the 0-based index of character N in its alphabet:

c28 = 6257896 + 36·10·27³·i1 + 10·27³·i2 + 27³·i3 + 27²·i4 + 27·i5 + i6

The standard range exactly fills 2²⁸ (37·36·10·27³ = 2²⁸ − 6257896). Unpacking is the mixed-radix inverse, strip spaces, undo the work-arounds; a receiver MUST reject a value whose decoded string does not re-pack to the same value (e.g. embedded spaces, 11ABC).

§2.4 Body encodings

6-bit text (body_kind 0). Each character is a 6-bit index into CHARSET6:

index characters
0 space
1–26 A–Z
27–36 0–9
37–58 . , ? / - + = : ; ' " ! @ # ( ) * & % < > _ (in this order)
59–63 $ [ ] ~ and newline (LF, U+000A)

As a Python string literal: " ABCDEFGHIJKLMNOPQRSTUVWXYZ0123456789.,?/-+=:;'\"!@#()*&%<>_$[]~\n". Lower-case letters are converted to upper case by the sender; any other character requires UTF-8. The body-length field counts characters; the body occupies 6·N bits. The design baseline is a 32-character 6-bit body (192 bits).

UTF-8 text (body_kind 1). The UTF-8 bytes, 8 bits each; length counts bytes (≤ 255). A receiver MUST reject invalid UTF-8.

Binary (body_kind 2). Raw bytes, 8 bits each; length counts bytes (≤ 255). Used only for the structured control-frame bodies of section 4, every one of which is a published, non-secret format (section 0). A SUBMIT body is never binary: message content must be 6-bit or UTF-8 text, and mailboxes reject a binary SUBMIT (4.2, bad-encoding), because an opaque byte string is not a publicly documented message encoding (§97.309(a)(4)) and could carry ciphertext (§97.113(a)(4)).

The reference implementation picks 6-bit text automatically only when the text is already exactly representable in CHARSET6 (lossless); senders may choose to upper-case to save airtime.

§2.5 CRC-16

CRC-16/CCITT-FALSE: polynomial 0x1021, initial value 0xFFFF, no input/output reflection, final XOR 0x0000 (check value: "123456789" → 0x29B1).

Input: the bit string header ‖ body ‖ tag (the CRC slot itself excluded), packed MSB-first and zero-padded to a whole byte. The 16-bit result is inserted MSB first between body and tag. The CRC confirms the LDPC decode (section 3); it is not an authenticator.

§3. Coding / size classes

Reference implementation: weakmail/coded.py (this section) on top of weakmail/ldpc.py (code construction, encoder, decoder).

§3.1 Code

Every frame is protected by one rate-½ (3,6)-regular LDPC code (design doc, "Framing and coding"). The parity-check matrix H for length n is LDPCCode.make(n, seed=0) and the bit interleaver is interleaver(n, seed=0) (weakmail/ldpc.py: PEG-style construction driven by a counter-based SplitMix64 hash, so both are exact functions of (n, seed) on every platform). The construction and the interleaver are defined normatively, in integer arithmetic, in Appendix A; the reference digests of every class are in 3.6. Seed 0 is part of this specification; changing it, the construction, or the table in 3.2 changes what goes on air. The encoder is systematic: the information bits occupy the codeword positions info_positions (the non-pivot columns of H after GF(2) row reduction, ascending), in order.

§3.3 Encoding

  1. u = the frame's information bits (section 2).
  2. n = smallest class with n/2 ≥ len(u); append n/2 − len(u) zero bits after the tag.
  3. c = systematic LDPC codeword of the padded u under H(n, seed 0).
  4. Transmit tx[i] = c[perm[i]], perm = interleaver(n, 0).
  5. tx is cut into 3-bit groups MSB-first and mapped onto tones (section 1), after the 8-symbol preamble.

§3.4 Decoding (candidate lengths)

The receiver does not know n before decoding (the length field is inside the codeword). For each candidate class n (by default every class with n ≤ the number of available LLRs, in ascending order; a receiver may narrow the list using the burst end found by its carrier tracker): take the first n bit LLRs after the preamble, de-interleave (L_cw[perm[i]] = L_tx[i]), run normalised min-sum (60 iterations, α = 0.8, early exit on a zero syndrome), then accept the first candidate for which all of these hold:

  1. the syndrome is zero;
  2. the CRC-16 of section 2.5 matches;
  3. the header is valid (version 1, defined type and body kind, decodable callsigns, valid UTF-8) and every padding bit after the tag is zero;
  4. the decoded frame's own information length selects exactly class n (3.2).

Informative (receiver robustness): the reference decoder saturates its input LLRs at ±3 × the median non-zero |LLR| before min-sum (ldpc.LLR_SAT_REL). Min-sum is invariant to a global LLR scale, and so is this relative cap, but it is not robust to single outliers: a square-law metric makes one impulse- or carrier-hit symbol produce wrong-sign LLRs many times the typical size, which three check messages can never overrule. The cap costs nothing measurable on clean frames.

Check 4 means a frame is only ever accepted at the length its sender must have used. In the reference tests, 920 + 3 600 decodes of real frames at wrong candidate lengths (noiseless and 2.5 dB Eb/N0 LLRs, trailing noise) never reached a zero syndrome, let alone a CRC pass.

§4. Mailbox protocol

This section defines what each frame type carries and how a mailbox reacts to it. It sits on top of section 2: everything here deals with decoded frames, with no DSP involved. The reference implementation is weakmail/mailbox/ (protocol.py, keys.py, store.py). The design doc sources are "Authentication and mailbox protocol" and "Regulatory envelope".

Conventions: every control-frame body is binary (body_kind 2, ≤ 255 bytes). Integers are big-endian. A callsign inside a body is its c28 value (section 2.3) in 4 bytes, with the top 4 bits zero. msg_id, key_id and nonces are 8 bytes, signatures 64 bytes. All bodies are published, non-secret formats (section 0).

§4.2 Receive pipeline

For every decoded frame a mailbox runs these steps in order. It writes exactly one log entry per frame (4.11) and returns the frames it will transmit.

  1. Own frame: if src is the mailbox's own callsign, ignore it (own-frame). The one exception is a control command (step 3): the licensee is often the control operator and may sign CTL commands under the station's own callsign; the control-key signature, not src, authorises a command.
  2. Timestamp window: rebuild the full minute count as the value congruent to the 16-bit field (mod 65536) that is nearest the mailbox clock. Reject if it is more than 2880 minutes (±2 days) away (stale-timestamp). Because the window is far smaller than the about ±32768-minute (~22.7-day) range where the nearest value is unambiguous (2.1), wrapping of the 16-bit field is handled exactly. The same check applies to the original SUBMIT inside a relay envelope.
  3. Control command: a SUBMIT to the mailbox's callsign whose text starts with CTL is handled under 4.11.
  4. Halted: a halted mailbox logs the frame (halted) and does nothing else.
  5. Dispatch by type (4.3–4.10, 4.17). Frames whose dst is neither the mailbox nor ANY are ignored (not-addressed). SUBMIT, KEY and SIG are the exceptions (any mailbox that hears them acts on them), and so are LIST/CHALLENGE/DELIVER/BEACON from other mailboxes (4.9). A body that breaks this section's formats is logged as malformed.

Before step 1 a mailbox runs its time-driven housekeeping (4.18): bodies whose detached SIG never came are released or dropped, relays out of attempts are declared failed, and periodically the store is pruned.

SUBMIT (relayed = 0):

  • A token destination is rejected (bad-dst).
  • A body that is not 6-bit or UTF-8 text is rejected and logged (bad-encoding; 2.4).
  • If the msg_id has been seen before, the frame is a duplicate. It is not stored again, but while the mailbox still holds the message it re-sends the ACK, in case the submitter missed the first one — at most 2 times per msg_id, so replays cannot make the mailbox transmit without bound (duplicate). The dedup cache outlives purges.
  • The originator's tag is then checked:
tag key situation outcome
none — stored, UNVERIFIED + UNSIGNED
HMAC mailbox has the group key, tag valid stored, VERIFIED_HMAC
HMAC mailbox has the group key, tag invalid rejected, logged (bad-sig)
HMAC mailbox has no group key stored, UNVERIFIED
BLS — (reserved in v1) rejected, logged (malformed): an unspecified 48-byte field would be stored and re-broadcast verbatim, which is not a publicly documented encoding (the reason BINARY bodies are refused, 2.4)
Ed25519 trusted key (4.10) verifies stored, VERIFIED_DIRECTORY / VERIFIED_TOFU, key_id of that key
Ed25519 a pending changed key verifies stored, UNVERIFIED + KEY_CHANGED, key_id of the pending key
Ed25519 trusted key exists, nothing verifies rejected, logged (bad-sig)
Ed25519 no key known stored, UNVERIFIED, key_id all-zero

Bad signatures are never stored. A mailbox configured "verified only" (the stricter reading of the design doc's Verify rule) logs unverified frames as rejected-unverified and does not store them. Anti-flooding: a mailbox holds at most 48 un-acknowledged unverified messages per addressee (one full LIST window); further unverified SUBMITs (direct or relayed) to that addressee are logged as rejected-unverified ("unverified quota") and not stored. Verified messages are never capped.

An untagged SUBMIT is first matched against held SIG frames from the same source (4.17): if one names it, it is stored with the status that SIG gives. Otherwise, if it announces a SIG (more_follows = 1), it is stored as held (stored-pending): not listed, not counted against the unverified quota, at most 48 held per addressee — this also applies to a "verified only" mailbox. Without either, the 4.2 table applies.

When a message is stored (or held), the mailbox replies ACK(body = msg_id) to src.

§4.3 Status byte

LIST entries and envelopes carry one status byte:

bits meaning
1–0 auth: 0 UNVERIFIED, 1 VERIFIED_DIRECTORY, 2 VERIFIED_TOFU, 3 VERIFIED_HMAC
2 KEY_CHANGED: the originator's key changed at this mailbox (4.10)
3 RELAYED: the message reached this mailbox through a relay
4 UNSIGNED: the original frame has no tag and no detached SIG
5 DETACHED_SIG: authenticated by a separate SIG frame (4.17); in an envelope, the SIG blob follows the body blob (4.7)
6 HELD (LIST only): the querier's current key may not retrieve or ACK this message — it was stored before that TOFU key was pinned (4.10). Never set in an envelope (reject).
7 reserved, 0 (reject otherwise)

In LIST and DELIVER, KEY_CHANGED is worked out when the frame is sent: it is set if the message was signed by a pending key, or if the originator's callsign is flagged as key-changed at that moment. Superseded keys: a message stored as VERIFIED_DIRECTORY or VERIFIED_TOFU whose key_id is no longer the key the mailbox trusts for its originator (for example a TOFU pin later overridden by the directory) is listed and delivered as UNVERIFIED + KEY_CHANGED, and is never relayed, regardless of acknowledgement or the 7-day notice. The exception is a key that moved to the currently trusted key through endorsed rotations (key-rotated, 4.8/4.10).

§4.4 LIST

Body: count(1) ‖ count × entry. Each entry is 21 bytes: msg_id(8) ‖ originator c28(4) ‖ key_id(8) ‖ status(1). That allows at most 12 entries per frame (1 + 12·21 = 253 bytes). A QUERY is answered with every un-acknowledged, non-held message addressed to the querier, oldest first, up to the LIST cap (default 48 = 4 frames; configurable 1–192). When more are pending, the capped set is chosen verified-first (oldest first within the verified and the unverified group) and then listed oldest first, so unverified junk cannot hide verified mail. The messages are split across LIST frames with more_follows, and an empty result is a LIST with count = 0.

QUERY rate limit. QUERY is unsigned, so anyone can make a mailbox transmit a LIST (and a CHALLENGE) by sending one under any callsign. A mailbox answers at most 4 QUERYs per querier callsign and at most 30 in total per sliding 600 s window (both configurable); further QUERYs are logged as rate-limited and get no reply. The total bound matters because the per-callsign bound alone is defeated by spoofing many callsigns. The key_id is the key that verified the message (for a detached signature: the key that verified the SIG), or the pending key that signed it, or all-zero if no key is known. A recipient who sees an unfamiliar key_id for a known correspondent, or the KEY_CHANGED bit, has spotted a key change. The HELD bit tells the recipient that a listed message cannot be fetched with its current key (4.10).

§4.5 CHALLENGE (one per QUERY session)

Body: nonce(8) ‖ list_digest(8), where

list_digest = SHA-256("WM1-LIST" ‖ msg_id_1 ‖ … ‖ msg_id_n)[0:8]      (LIST order)

The nonce is 64 bits from a CSPRNG and is valid for 600 s (10 minutes) after issue. A challenge goes out only when at least one message is listed, the querier has a trusted key (4.10), and that key may claim at least one listed message (not every entry HELD). A recipient with no key gets the LIST alone, so they know to announce a key. The digest covers every listed msg_id, held ones included, so a client can recompute it from the LIST. At most the 8 newest nonces per querier are retained. Design choice: one challenge per session, with responses per message or per batch. One CHALLENGE (cheap downlink) covers everything listed in the session. Each RESPONSE (an expensive weak-signal uplink of 684 info bits) retrieves either one message or the whole list.

§4.6 RESPONSE

Body: selector(8) ‖ sig(64), where selector is one listed msg_id or the session's list_digest, and sig is the section 5.5 challenge signature with msg_id := selector:

sig = Ed25519-Sign(sk_recipient, "WM1-CHAL" ‖ nonce ‖ selector ‖ ASCII(mailbox_call))

The body leaves out the nonce to save 64 uplink bits, so the mailbox looks up the matching challenge itself. Acceptance:

  1. src must have a trusted key, otherwise unknown-key. Pending keys are never used here.
  2. The mailbox tries every retained nonce issued to src, newest first, and picks the one whose signature verifies. If none does: bad-sig. This also covers a signature made for another mailbox, another station's nonce, or the wrong key.
  3. If (nonce, selector) has already been accepted: replay (logged, nothing sent).
  4. If the time is past the nonce's expiry: expired-nonce.
  5. The selector must be the digest (meaning every message listed in that session) or a listed msg_id. Otherwise: bad-selector.
  6. The mailbox records (nonce, selector) as used, atomically. Each pair is accepted once. It then sends a DELIVER chain for each selected message that is still stored, addressed to src, and claimable by src's trusted key (not held, 4.10) (accepted-response; held messages are counted in the log detail).

Expired nonces are kept for a day, so a late replay is logged as a replay or an expiry rather than as a bad signature.

Delivery does not remove a message. It stays listed until the recipient sends an Ed25519-signed ACK naming its msg_id, so a DELIVER lost on air can be retried with a new QUERY. The mailbox ignores an unsigned delivery ACK and logs a badly signed one as bad-sig, so nobody can spoof a delivery ACK. A held message (4.10) is not acknowledged by a valid ACK either (ignored).

§4.7 DELIVER, envelopes and fragmentation

The blob of a frame is its information bits (header ‖ body ‖ CRC ‖ tag, section 2), packed MSB-first and zero-padded to a whole byte. That is exactly what was received. The mailbox stores the blob of every SUBMIT and delivers it byte for byte. The recipient decodes it with the section 2 rules, finds the originator's untouched signature inside it, and gets msg_id back from it. The blob has to be carried inside the body like this because type, flags and tag kind are covered by the originator's signature: turning a SUBMIT into a DELIVER would otherwise break it.

Envelope:

envelope = status(1) ‖ n_hops(1) ‖ blob_len(2) ‖ blob
           [‖ sig_len(2) ‖ sig_blob]          present iff status bit DETACHED_SIG
           ‖ n_hops × hop_record(72)

sig_blob is the blob of the SIG frame that authenticates an untagged body (4.17), byte for byte as received, so the recipient can verify the originator end to end. A split-signed 32-character message (38-byte body blob, 94-byte SIG blob) has a 138-byte envelope: one DELIVER frame of 1220 information bits.

The envelope is split into fragment bodies of the form frag(1) ‖ chunk(≤ 254), with frag = index << 4 | last_index (at most 16 fragments). Each fragment goes out as one DELIVER frame, with more_follows = 1 on every fragment except the last. A signed 32-character SUBMIT has a 102-byte blob, so its DELIVER body is 107 bytes: one frame. The largest possible signed SUBMIT (333-byte blob) takes two.

§4.8 KEY

Body: pk(32), or pk(32) ‖ rotation_sig(64). The frame must carry an Ed25519 tag made by pk itself. That tag is the self-signature, and through the canonical bytes it covers the callsign (src), the timestamp and the key. If the self-signature fails, the frame is bad-sig and nothing is pinned. A valid KEY is ACKed (body = KEY frame msg_id) and handled as in 4.10. The result is logged as key-pinned, key-same, key-directory-match, key-changed, key-directory-conflict or key-rotated.

KEY frames are de-duplicated by msg_id like SUBMITs: a KEY frame seen before is logged as duplicate and is neither re-processed nor ACKed, so replaying recorded announcements cannot make the mailbox transmit. A station that missed the ACK sends a fresh KEY frame (new timestamp, hence new msg_id); re-announcing the same key is harmless (key-same).

rotation_sig = Ed25519-Sign(sk_old, "WM1-KROT" ‖ c28(src)(4) ‖ pk_new(32))

Info-bit sizes: KEY 876 bits, KEY with rotation 1388 bits.

§4.9 Relay

A mailbox may relay. Relaying is off by default; enabling it is an operator decision under §97.113(d) / §97.221, and the design doc's risk register says "keep the mailbox attended, or drop relay". The relay is triggered when the mailbox hears another mailbox P's LIST, CHALLENGE or DELIVER addressed to station X, or P's BEACON naming X. The peer frame must carry a valid Ed25519 tag from P's trusted key (4.10); an untagged or unverifiable peer frame is logged as peer-heard ("unauthenticated peer frame") and creates no heard-via entry and no relay. The mailbox then treats X as reachable via P for 24 h, and on hearing an authenticated LIST or BEACON from P it forwards eligible messages addressed to X (or to P itself).

Eligible means all of the following: - auth is VERIFIED_DIRECTORY or VERIFIED_TOFU. Unverified messages are never relayed, and neither are HMAC-only ones, since those are not publicly verifiable. - No key change is flagged. - Every earlier hop verified. - The message has not been acknowledged by its recipient. - It has fewer than 3 hops, P is not already on the path, and it has not already been relayed to P.

The relaying mailbox re-originates the message under its own callsign: SUBMIT, relayed = 1, src = relaying mailbox, dst = P, body = fragmented envelope (4.7). The envelope carries the original blob unchanged, the relay's status byte, and the hop records with a new one appended:

hop_record = c28(call)(4) ‖ time_min(4, full minutes since epoch) ‖ hop_sig(64)
hop_sig    = Ed25519-Sign(sk_call, "WM1-HOP" ‖ msg_id ‖ earlier hop_records ‖ c28(call) ‖ time_min)

The hop signature sits in a separate field of the envelope, and it chains the earlier records so the path order is signed too. The outer frame has no tag. Each Ed25519 hop adds 576 bits (512 signature + 64 call/time).

The receiving mailbox checks, in order, each of the following and rejects with the code shown: - relay fragments arrive in order (index 0, 1, …, at most 16); a missing, repeated or excess fragment drops the partial chain: malformed; - the envelope carries an original (non-relayed) SUBMIT: malformed; - the original SUBMIT is addressed to a station (not a token): bad-dst; and was not originated by the receiving mailbox itself: own-frame; - the last hop's call equals the outer src, with at most 3 hops: bad-relay; - the receiver is not already on the path: relay-loop; - every hop signature verifies against the hop station's trusted key: bad-sig. A hop station with no known key is not an error; it is noted, and the message then becomes ineligible for further relay.

It then applies the encoding (bad-encoding), window and dedup checks to the original SUBMIT and verifies the originator itself, by the 4.2 table — or, when the envelope carries a detached SIG, by that SIG (4.17; a SIG that does not name the body from the same source is malformed) — ignoring the incoming status byte. The message is stored with RELAYED set and the hop records (and SIG blob) kept, and the receiver sends the relay-ACK to the relaying mailbox.

Relay-ACK. An ACK frame with src = the receiving mailbox P, dst = the relaying mailbox R, body = the original msg_id(s), Ed25519-tagged by P's trusted key (so a mailbox that accepts relayed mail must have a signing key). R closes the relay for (msg_id, P) only on a valid relay-ACK; an unsigned one is ignored and a badly signed one is bad-sig.

Retries. A relayed message that is not relay-ACKed is retried: the n-th attempt is followed by a wait of 600 s · 2^(n−1) (at most 4 h), after which the next authenticated LIST or BEACON heard from P triggers a retry (with a fresh hop record). After 4 attempts and the last wait, the relay is given up and logged as a relay-failed event. Attempt count and backoff are configurable. A late relay-ACK is still recorded.

Global bound. A peer needs only a trusted key, and a TOFU pin (4.10) comes from a single KEY frame, so every freshly announced callsign could pose as a new peer and trigger its own relay of every eligible message. A relaying mailbox therefore sends at most 60 relayed SUBMIT frames (fragments) in any 3600 s, over all peers; once the bound is reached the remaining messages wait for the next trigger after the window has moved (relay-limited event). A peers allow-list (configuration) removes TOFU-only peers altogether.

§4.10 Key directory and TOFU

Callsign → key trust, in priority order:

  1. Directory: a JSON document signed by the directory operator. It is authoritative.
  2. TOFU pin: the key from the first valid KEY frame heard for a callsign that has no directory entry.
  3. Otherwise the callsign is unknown.

A KEY frame that differs from (1) or (2) is a key change. The mailbox logs it as an event (key-conflict against the directory, key-change against a pin) and holds the new key as pending. It never pins the new key silently. Pending keys only ever let a message be stored as UNVERIFIED + KEY_CHANGED. They never authorise a RESPONSE or a relay.

There is one exception: a KEY frame with a rotation_sig that the currently pinned TOFU key verifies moves the pin (key-rotated). Directory bindings cannot be rotated this way.

A callsign counts as key-changed if it has a pending key, or if it has a key-change event (key-change, key-conflict, key-rotated, directory-override, directory-removed) less than 7 days old that the control operator has not acknowledged. The control operator settles a change either by accepting a pending key, which re-pins it, or by rejecting all of them.

Directory document:

{"format": "weakmail-keydir", "version": 1, "issued": <unix seconds>,
 "operator": "<callsign>", "entries": [{"call": "K1ABC", "pk": "<64 hex>"}, ...],
 "signature": {"alg": "ed25519", "value": "<128 hex>"}}

Signature input: "WM1-DIR" ‖ JSON(document without "signature"), written as canonical JSON: keys sorted, no whitespace, ASCII only, and entries sorted by callsign. The mailbox rejects a document that is malformed, unsigned, signed under an algorithm it has no verifier for, badly signed, or older (issued) than the one it already holds (rollback protection).

Loading a directory replaces any TOFU pin that disagrees with it and logs directory-override. A callsign that was in the previous directory but is missing from the new one is not reopened to first-come TOFU: its last directory key is kept as a TOFU pin and the change is logged as directory-removed, which counts as a key-change event (flagged for 7 days or until acknowledged). Revocation is done by binding a new key, or by the control operator. Events are time-stamped with the load time (the wall clock by default). At most 4 distinct pending keys are held per callsign. The design doc's authoritative signer is the operator's LoTW/TQSL certificate. Its algorithm name tqsl-x509 is reserved, and the interface is stubbed (LotwTqslVerifier), because verifying a TQSL certificate chain is out of scope for v1.

Known TOFU limit: whoever announces first wins a callsign that has no directory entry. The directory is the fix.

Mail stored before a TOFU pin. Because of that limit, a TOFU key must not collect mail that arrived before it was pinned — that mail was addressed to the callsign, not to whoever announced first. Every pin records a claim time; a stored message whose receive time is earlier than the claim time of the addressee's current key is held for that key: it is listed with the HELD bit, but it is not delivered in answer to a RESPONSE signed by that key, and an ACK signed by that key does not remove it. Claim times:

how the key became trusted claim time
directory binding none — covers all mail
first TOFU pin (key-pinned) the pin time
endorsed rotation (key-rotated) inherited from the endorsing key
control operator accepts a pending key the acceptance time, or none if the operator also approves earlier mail
directory binding removed (directory-removed) none (it was authoritative)
control operator approves earlier mail (key-approved-prior) none

So a recipient should announce a key (or get into the directory) before others send them mail; otherwise the control operator must vouch for them.

§4.11 Accountability and control

The mailbox logs every frame it receives or sends, verified or not, together with: - its full blob (the content log for unattended operation); - the decoder metadata (rx_meta, including snr_db); - one result code: stored-verified, stored-unverified, stored-pending (held for a detached SIG), bad-sig, bad-encoding, duplicate, stale-timestamp, bad-dst, rejected-unverified, listed, rate-limited, accepted-response, replay, expired-nonce, bad-selector, unknown-key, key-*, acked, ignored, peer-heard, relay-fragment, bad-relay, relay-loop, sig-applied, sig-pending, control, halted, not-addressed, own-frame, malformed, or sent for transmitted frames.

Key events, purges, halts and resumes go in an event log, as do sig-timeout (a held body's SIG never came), sig-expired (a SIG's bodies never came), relay-failed and key-approved-prior.

The control operator can purge a msg_id. Purging deletes the message, but the msg_id stays in the dedup cache, so replaying the SUBMIT cannot bring the message back. The control operator can also halt the mailbox (the kill switch). A halted mailbox stores nothing and answers nothing, but it keeps logging. A CW ID still owed for transmissions made before the halt may be sent (4.12). The halt survives restarts.

Over the air, a control command is a SUBMIT to the mailbox's callsign. It must be Ed25519-signed by the configured control-operator key, and its text body is one of:

CTL HALT        CTL RESUME        CTL PURGE <16 hex digits of msg_id>

Control commands go through the normal timestamp window and msg_id dedup. In addition, HALT and RESUME must have a strictly increasing timestamp: the mailbox keeps the full minute count of the last accepted HALT/RESUME and logs an older-or-equal one as replay without acting on it, so a recorded command the mailbox never decoded cannot be replayed after a later one (send kill-switch commands at least one minute apart). PURGE is not ordered (replaying it can only delete again). A command signed by the control key is honoured even when its src is the mailbox's own callsign (4.2 step 1). An unsigned or forged command is logged as bad-sig. Commands are honoured even while the mailbox is halted, so RESUME works. HALT gets no reply; the others are ACKed.

§4.12 Station identification

Every frame header carries the sender's callsign in ASCII-decodable c28. On top of that, the mailbox keys a CW ID DE <call> at ≤ 20 wpm (§97.119). The schedule is a pure function:

  • With no transmission since the last ID, no ID is owed.
  • Otherwise the ID is due at max(first_tx_since_last_id, last_id + 600 s). Having never sent an ID counts as having sent it long ago.

In practice that means an ID at least every 10 minutes while transmitting, and immediately on the first transmission after more than 10 minutes of silence. The end-of-communication ID is up to the station daemon. (Informative: the reference mailbox daemon also ends every key-up that empties its queue with the ID, and when frames it keyed are followed by frames it cannot send, or a key-up fails, it keys a bare ID as soon as the channel allows; at shutdown it sends an ID still owed.)

§4.13 BEACON body

Frame type 8 (distinct from the OOK BEACON waveform of section 1). The body is a list of up to 63 c28 callsigns (4 bytes each) that the mailbox has heard directly in the last 24 h. Other mailboxes use it to make relay decisions (4.9).

§4.17 Detached signatures (SIG frames)

The design doc's E9 options "body first, signature second" and "batch signing" are one mechanism. A body frame is an original SUBMIT with tag kind 0 (normally with more_follows = 1 to announce the SIG). A SIG frame (type 9, src = the originator, dst = ANY or the mailbox, binary body, tag kind 3) carries

body   = digest_1 ‖ … ‖ digest_n               1 ≤ n ≤ 15, distinct
digest = SHA-256(canonical_bytes(body frame))[0:16]
tag    = Ed25519-Sign(sk_originator, canonical_bytes(SIG frame))   (5.1)

The first 8 bytes of a digest are the body's msg_id (an untagged frame's msg_id is SHA-256(canonical_bytes)[0:8], 5.3). One digest is a split message; several, from one sender in one session, are a batch signature. 128-bit digests keep a substituted body at 2¹²⁸ work and a signer-made colliding pair at 2⁶⁴, where a bare 64-bit msg_id would allow 2³² collisions. The digests only name bodies that are themselves sent in the clear: a SIG never stands in for content, and a SIG whose bodies never arrive authenticates nothing (section 0).

Mailbox processing of a SIG (after the 4.2 steps 1–4):

  1. Tag kind other than Ed25519, or a body not of the format above: malformed.
  2. msg_id seen before: duplicate (re-ACKed at most 2 times while held).
  3. Verify the SIG with the 4.2 table (trusted key, pending key, no key). Bad: bad-sig, nothing changes. Unverifiable on a "verified only" mailbox: rejected-unverified.
  4. Every stored message named by a digest — same src, tag kind 0, digest matching its canonical bytes, not already authenticated by a verified SIG — is upgraded: auth and key_id from the SIG, UNSIGNED cleared, DETACHED_SIG set, released if held, the SIG blob stored with it. (An unverifiable SIG does not release a held body past the unverified quota.)
  5. Digests naming no stored message are awaited: the SIG is held for up to 3600 s. When a matching untagged body arrives it is stored with the SIG's status (4.2). At most 4 unverifiable SIGs per source and 64 in total are held.
  6. Result sig-applied (all named bodies present) or sig-pending (some awaited), and ACK(body = SIG msg_id) to src. A SIG naming only messages it may not upgrade is ignored.

A body held for its SIG (4.2) that gets none within 3600 s becomes an ordinary UNVERIFIED + UNSIGNED message (subject to the unverified quota), or is dropped on a "verified only" mailbox (sig-timeout event). The ACK of a SIG does not say whether its bodies were all present, and a re-sent SIG is a duplicate that is not processed again, so a sender that re-sends a body more than 3600 s after its SIG was ACKed must send a fresh SIG naming the re-sent bodies (a new frame, hence a new msg_id; StationSession.resend does this), or the body is stored unsigned. Split-signed messages are relayed with their SIG blob in the envelope (4.7, 4.9), so the next mailbox and the recipient verify the originator themselves.

Airtime (coded bits; WEAK seconds at 6 bps), 32-character 6-bit messages:

scheme coded bits WEAK vs. Ed25519 each
1 message, Ed25519 tag 1626 271 s —
1 message, split (600 + SIG 1626) 2226 371 s +37 %
4 messages, Ed25519 each 6504 1084 s —
4 messages, batch (4 × 600 + SIG 2394) 4794 799 s −26 %
8 messages, batch (8 × 600 + SIG 4302) 9102 1517 s −30 %
15 messages, batch (15 × 600 + SIG 5322) 14322 2387 s −41 %

A single split message costs more airtime in total; what it buys is a short body frame (600 vs 1626 coded bits) that gets through when a long frame would not, with the signature retried separately and never forcing the body to be resent. Batch signing saves airtime from two messages up. E9 measures whether either is worth the complexity.

§4.19 Key cards (wmkey:)

Stations exchange public keys off air as key cards (text, or the same text as a QR code). A card is self-signed: it proves the card was made by the holder of the secret key, not that the callsign belongs to that holder (compare the key id in person, or use the directory).

card    = "wmkey:" ‖ BASE32(payload)          RFC 4648 base32, upper case, no padding
payload = version(1) = 1 ‖ flags(1) ‖ c28(callsign)(4, big-endian) ‖ pk(32) ‖ sig(64)
                                                                          (102 bytes)
sig     = Ed25519-Sign(sk, "WM1-WMKEY" ‖ version ‖ flags ‖ c28 ‖ pk)
flags   = bit 0: the key belongs to a mailbox; bits 1-7 zero

A reader accepts the scheme in any case, ignores whitespace (a QR reader's newline), upper-cases the data, and rejects a card whose length is not 102 bytes, whose version is not 1, whose reserved flag bits are set, whose callsign is not a standard c28 call (or is a token: ANY, DE, QRZ), or whose self-signature does not verify. The base32 alphabet and an upper-case WMKEY: prefix are in the QR alphanumeric set. "WM1-WMKEY" cannot collide with a frame's canonical bytes ("WM1" is followed by a byte ≥ 0x40 there, and - is 0x2D) or with another prefix of 4.14.

§5. Authentication

§5.1 Canonical encoding (signature input)

canonical_bytes = "WM1" (0x57 0x4D 0x31) ‖ pack_msb_first(header ‖ body)

The 92 header bits and the body bits are concatenated, packed MSB-first and zero-padded to a whole byte, and prefixed with the 3-byte ASCII magic WM1. The CRC and tag are not included. The tag-kind field is included, so the header that is signed always states the tag kind that is sent: a signer sets tag kind first, then signs. Because every header field is covered — version, type, source, destination, timestamp, flags (including relayed and more_follows) and body — nothing that is signed can be altered by a relay.

§5.2 Tag kinds

kind name tag bytes computation
0 none 0 —
1 HMAC-SHA256/64 8 HMAC-SHA256(K, canonical_bytes)[0:8], K a pre-shared group key
2 BLS12-381 48 reserved; format to be specified (see below)
3 Ed25519 64 Ed25519-Sign(sk, canonical_bytes) (RFC 8032, pure Ed25519)

BLS12-381 stays reserved in v1. Its attractions (a 384-bit signature and, above all, aggregation of relay hop signatures, design doc "Relay") need a pairing library, and there is no maintained, production-grade pure-Python BLS12-381 implementation (the pure-Python reference code is far too slow for a mailbox on a Pi and not intended for production use). The plan is to bind blst (the audited C/assembly library named in the design doc) later and then specify the format (G1 or G2 signatures, hash-to-curve suite, aggregation of hop records). Until then a BLS-tagged frame still decodes (the 48-byte slot is parsed), but a mailbox rejects a BLS-tagged SUBMIT (direct or relayed) as malformed and stores nothing (4.2): retransmitting an unspecified 384-bit field would be an undocumented encoding on air.

Ed25519 keys are the raw 32-byte RFC 8032 seed (private) and the 32-byte encoded point (public). HMAC tags are compared in constant time. HMAC offers no public verifiability: anyone holding K can forge, and an outsider must do ~2⁶⁴ work per forgery attempt.

§5.3 Message ID

msg_id = SHA-256(canonical_bytes ‖ tag)[0:8]

(the "first 64 bits of SHA-256 over the signed blob"). Mailboxes deduplicate by msg_id. An unsigned frame's msg_id is SHA-256(canonical_bytes)[0:8].

§5.4 Key ID

key_id = SHA-256(pk)[0:8]      (pk = 32-byte Ed25519 public key)

Carried in LIST frames so recipients notice a key change.

Detached-signature digests (SHA-256(canonical_bytes)[0:16] of an untagged frame) are defined in 4.17.

§5.5 Challenge-response (retrieval gate)

The mailbox issues a fresh 64-bit nonce from a CSPRNG (10-minute lifetime, accepted once). The recipient answers with a 64-byte Ed25519 signature over

"WM1-CHAL" (8 ASCII bytes) ‖ nonce (8) ‖ msg_id (8) ‖ ASCII(upper(mailbox_call))

where mailbox_call is the mailbox's callsign as text (e.g. W1MBX), stripped of whitespace and upper-cased. The response binds the nonce, the specific message and the specific mailbox, so it cannot be replayed for another message, at another mailbox, or after the nonce is spent. The WM1-CHAL prefix keeps challenge signatures disjoint from frame signatures: a frame's canonical bytes are WM1 followed by a header byte in 0x40–0x7F (version bits 01), never - (0x2D), so no signed message can be both.

            ok[c] = cap[c] > 0 and not forbidden[c]
            if no c is ok: return FAIL
            score[c] = ((near[c] ? 0 : 1) << 40) | (cap[c] << 34) | rnd[c]   if ok[c]
                       -1                                                     otherwise
            c* = the smallest index c with the maximum score
            append c* to chosen
            for every d with adj[c*][d]:          # neighbours of c* (before this variable)
                forbidden[d] = true                  # would close a 4-cycle
                near[e] = true for every e with adj[d][e]   # would close a 6-cycle
            forbidden[c*] = true
        for each of the 3 chosen checks c: cap[c] -= 1
        for each pair (a, b) of chosen checks: adj[a][b] = adj[b][a] = true
        var_checks[j] = chosen                    # in selection order
    if any cap[c] != 0: return FAIL
    return var_checks

H[c][j] = 1 iff c is one of var_checks[j]. Every published class succeeds on attempt 0. For n = 600 the first rows are var_checks[0..3] = (10,171,78), (59,31,190), (126,4,132), (270,65,213).

A.3 Systematic encoder positions. Reduce H over GF(2) to reduced row-echelon form, scanning columns 0..n-1 left to right (any pivoting order gives the same pivot columns). The pivot columns are the parity positions; the remaining columns, ascending, are info_positions (n = 600: 199, 296, 299, 300, 304, 305, 306, 307, ...). The codeword puts the information bits on info_positions in order; the parity bits are then the unique solution of H c = 0.

A.4 Interleaver.

interleaver(n, seed):
    key   = mix_key(DOMAIN_ILV, n, seed)
    k[i]  = splitmix64(key, i)            for i in 0..n-1
    perm  = indices 0..n-1 sorted by ascending k[i] (stable: ties by ascending i)
transmit: tx[i] = c[perm[i]]

§7. Downlink (mailbox → HT)

The downlink carries the mailbox's ACK, LIST, CHALLENGE and DELIVER frames to a recipient who hears them through an ordinary FM receiver, so it is decoded from audio, not IQ. A downlink frame is an ordinary weakmail frame (section 2): the same information bits, the same CRC, the same tag (the mailbox Ed25519-signs outbound frames when it has a key, 4.2). Only the waveform differs. A mailbox uses one configured waveform (downlink.waveform; relayed SUBMITs and BEACONs for other mailboxes use an MFSK class). Nothing on the air says which one, so a station must listen with its mailbox's waveform (station setting downlink, or weakmail station listen --downlink); a station may run several receivers.

§7.1 Transmission

Downlink audio contains no keying delay: the radio layer keys the transmitter, waits TX_DELAY_S and then plays the audio (radio.tx.transmit). The mailbox is half duplex and listens before it talks. It does not key while its receiver sees a carrier or a burst on the channel, while a burst is closing, while it expects a faded sender back (at most 60 s), or while the in-channel noise floor is raised by wideband energy (FM voice, data); the channel must then have been quiet for a configured time (default 3 s) counted only after the receiver has warmed up (about 5.5 s after start-up or after lost samples), and it never keys with a stalled receiver. It sends its CW identification as section 4.12 requires. The AX.25 source address also carries the mailbox callsign in plain ASCII. (Informative: the reference daemon, weakmail/daemon/scheduler.py, re-checks these conditions on the samples that arrived while it rendered a key-up's audio, and caps key-up length and hourly airtime.)

§7.2 AFSK1200 (normative)

Modulation. Bell 202: mark 1200 Hz, space 2200 Hz, 1200 baud, continuous phase. NRZI: a 0 bit changes the tone, a 1 bit keeps it. The level before the first bit is mark, so the first tone sent (the first flag's leading 0) is space (receivers must not depend on it).

HDLC. Each frame is preceded and followed by the flag 0x7E. Consecutive frames in one transmission share a single flag between them. Inside a frame (address through FCS) a 0 is inserted after every five consecutive 1 bits. Bytes are sent least-significant bit first. A transmission starts with at least 24 flags (160 ms, for the receiver's clock and slicer) and ends with at least 4.

AX.25 v2 UI frame.

field bytes content
destination 7 frame.dst (a callsign, or ANY/DE/QRZ): six ASCII characters, space padded, each shifted left one bit; then the SSID byte 1 1 1 SSID(4) 0 (C bit = 1: command). A 7-character call (Eswatini 3DA0xxx, 2.3) is written in its packed form 3D0xxx
source 7 frame.src, same encoding; SSID byte 0 1 1 SSID(4) 1 (extension bit = 1: last address)
control 1 0x03 (UI, P = 0)
PID 1 0xF0 (no layer 3)
info 1–256 see below
FCS 2 CRC-16/X.25 over address…info, low byte first

CRC-16/X.25 is the reflected polynomial 0x1021 (0x8408 in reflected form), initial value 0xFFFF and final XOR 0xFFFF. Its check value is crc(b"123456789") = 0x906E, and the register over a received frame including a good FCS (without the final XOR) is 0xF0B8. SSIDs default to 0 and carry no weakmail meaning. Digipeater addresses may appear on receive and are ignored.

Info field. The blob of a weakmail frame is its information bits header ‖ body ‖ CRC-16 ‖ tag (section 2, info_length bits i_0 … i_{L-1} in on-air order) packed MSB-first into bytes and zero-padded at the end. Byte j is Σ_{k=0..7} i_{8j+k} · 2^(7-k), with i_m = 0 for m ≥ L. That is exactly the blob of section 4.7. HDLC then sends each byte LSB first, so the bits of byte j go on air in the order i_{8j+7}, i_{8j+6}, …, i_{8j}.

  • If the blob is at most 256 bytes (AX.25's default N1), the info field is the blob. Its first byte is 01xxxxxx (version 1).
  • A longer blob is cut into chunks of 254 bytes (the last one may be shorter). Chunk index (0-based) goes out in its own UI frame, whose info field is 0xC0 | index << 3 | last_index (one byte) ‖ SHA-256(blob)[0] (one byte, the group check) ‖ chunk. The top bits 11 cannot begin a version-1 blob. At most 8 fragments are allowed. The fragments go out in index order, back to back, in one transmission.

The largest frames the mailbox sends are a full DELIVER fragment (255-byte body plus Ed25519 tag: a 2660-bit or 333-byte blob) and a 12-entry LIST (2644 bits, 331 bytes). Each takes two AX.25 frames. Every other mailbox frame (ACK, CHALLENGE, a short LIST, the DELIVER of a signed 32-character SUBMIT) fits one. AX.25 fragmentation sits below the weakmail frame and is separate from the DELIVER more_follows chains of 4.7.

Receiver rules. The receiver accepts only frames whose FCS is good, whose control is UI and whose PID is 0xF0. It ignores other traffic (APRS and the like). It parses the info field as a blob or a fragment by its top two bits, and groups fragments by (source, destination, group check byte, last_index). A group is reassembled when every index is present. The receiver then checks that SHA-256(blob)[0] matches the group check byte and that the blob parses under section 2 (CRC-16, header validity, all-zero padding). It rejects the frame if the AX.25 source or destination differs from the address form (above) of the weakmail frame's src / dst.

Reference demodulator (informative), weakmail/downlink/afsk1200.py:

  1. resample to 12 kS/s and apply a zero-phase 700–2700 Hz band-pass;
  2. one-bit complex correlators at 1200 and 2200 Hz, taking magnitudes m, s;
  3. seven slicers g·m − s with g = −6 … +6 dB in 2 dB steps (pre-/de-emphasis twist);
  4. per slicer, a transition-driven first-order clock loop (gain 0.25 per zero crossing);
  5. NRZI decode, flag search, de-stuffing and FCS check;
  6. "fix bits": when the FCS fails but the region starts with two valid-looking AX.25 addresses, retry with the least confident level decisions inverted (16 single flips, then the 15 pairs among the 6 weakest);
  7. de-duplicate over slicers.

§7.3 MFSK downlink

The mailbox transmits the weakmail burst of section 1 (tones, Gray map, class preamble, LDPC, interleaver; sections 1–3) as audio for its FM transmitter, mfsk.audio at flat drive (preemph_comp only for a transmitter without pre-emphasis). MOBILE is the default and WEAK is optional. Several frames in one transmission are separate complete bursts with at least two symbols of silence between them.

Reference receiver (informative), weakmail/downlink/mfsk_audio.py. The FM receiver removes the carrier, so the audio has no frequency offset to search. In outline:

  1. resample to 8 kS/s and apply a zero-phase 1000–2800 Hz band-pass;
  2. one-symbol rectangular FFT energies at the eight tones (max over ±1 bin) every 1/8 symbol. A preamble candidate is a local maximum of the class pattern's mean tone share reaching 0.25 (noise: mean 0.125, s.d. 0.025);
  3. rank the size classes by where the per-symbol tone concentration drops, then by burst presence. Refine the timing to 1/32 symbol. For bursts long enough to drift, also search the sender's sound-card clock over ±400 ppm;
  4. divide each tone column by its median (this cancels the receiver's audio response, such as de-emphasis mismatch), then compute detect.soft_bits and run coded.decode_bits over the 5 best-ranked classes;
  5. mask the decoded burst and continue with the next candidate.
